# 10 Guidelines for Designing Your Site's AI Chatbots

## Summary

NN/g watched real users try AI chatbots on sites like Home Depot, Amazon, Williams Sonoma, Redfin and Turo. Small design choices decided whether people trusted the bot or gave up on it. The big lesson: a chatbot has to show what it can do and what it knows, because users won't guess.

## Key takeaways

- **One chat, not two.** Merge AI chat with existing support chat into a single entry point that can hand over to a human. Users shouldn't need to understand your internal setup
- **Follow the user.** Keep the bot available on every page of a journey, including checkout
- **Say what it can do.** Skip vague "ask me anything" greetings. List the topics it covers and tailor suggestions to the page the user is on
- **Show it's page aware.** If the bot knows what product the user is viewing or browsed earlier, make that obvious through its suggested questions
- **Buttons over text.** Offer suggested follow ups as clickable chips, keep them coming after each answer, and don't repeat ones the user ignored
- **Use images** for products and step by step instructions
- **Keep chats short** with expand and collapse details in place
- **Don't autoscroll** to the bottom of long, streaming answers. Keep the user at the top of the new message
- **Let users resize, save and share** the chat
- **Offer voice input.** It's also an accessibility win
