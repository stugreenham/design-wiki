# DESIGN.md: documenting design intent for AI

## Summary

Doug Lazarini argues that DESIGN.md could become one of the most important files in AI product work, alongside CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md. Instead of re-prompting an AI about spacing, type or accessibility every time, you write those expectations down once and every AI session inherits them. The bigger shift: design systems used to document components for humans, and now they document design intent for AI.

## Key takeaways

- **What a good DESIGN.md covers:**
  - Design principles and product philosophy
  - Visual language: spacing, colour, typography
  - Component usage and design system conventions
  - Interaction patterns, accessibility and motion
  - UX writing standards and content rules
- **Documentation becomes operational.** AI agents read it before they generate interfaces, review designs or write code. Better docs mean better output
- **Pair it with CLAUDE.md, Figma MCP and your design tokens,** so AI works from your product, not generic UI patterns
- **"From prompt engineering to systems engineering."** Great AI-made interfaces come from better context, not better prompts

## From the comments

- **Split big files** into focused ones (design system, accessibility, UX review, handoff), so the AI only loads what's relevant
- **Rules should be testable,** not just descriptive
- **Ownership matters.** One team found their file drifted as soon as engineers stopped opening it
